## Context

German mid-market SaaS grew 12% CAGR between 2022 and 2025, and the segment is now the largest addressable slice of EMEA (McKinsey 2025 benchmark). BfDI's 2026 enforcement priority list singles out foreign SaaS providers, and SAP has restructured its DACH mid-market coverage to defend against new entrants. A decision is required this cycle on whether to enter, and if so how.

## Key Question

Should we enter the German mid-market SaaS segment in 2027, and if so under which entry mode?

## Recommendation

Enter Germany in Q1 2027 via a bounded greenfield office capped at 15 staff for year one, owned by the Head of EMEA Sales with a first-referenceable-customer milestone at month nine. This choice preserves strategic control of the customer relationship, sits inside the Board's stated envelope, and avoids the SAP counter-offer that a broader beachhead would provoke.

## Supporting Evidence

The German mid-market SaaS segment grew 12% CAGR 2022-2025 and is now the largest addressable slice of EMEA (McKinsey Germany SaaS Benchmark 2025). BfDI's 2026 audit priority list singles out foreign SaaS entrants, which makes local counsel and a pre-filed DPIA a table stake rather than an optional cost (Reuters, June 2026). SAP has already restructured its DACH mid-market coverage to defend against new entrants, which shortens the window in which a bounded beachhead can settle in without triggering a bundled counter-offer (Bloomberg, Q4 2025).

## Risks and Mitigations

SAP's likely bundled counter-offer is the largest strategic risk; the mitigation is to pick a beachhead segment SAP under-serves and instrument competitive intelligence within thirty days of first customer signing. BfDI's audit posture is a regulatory table stake; the mitigation is to pre-file a Records of Processing description with a German subprocessor within the first ninety days.

## Next Steps

2. Local counsel and DPO engagement complete by Oct 31 (Head of EMEA Sales).
3. First customer signed by Q2 2027 (Head of EMEA Sales).

### CRIT-001 — risk-underestimated (critical)

**Steelmanned position:** The financial risk (RISK-003) understates the true CAC premium: sourcing shows a 55-70 percent premium band but the recommendation treats the low end of that band as the operating case, and no explicit gate handles the high-end scenario at month six or twelve.

**Implication if true:** The 18-month capital envelope would run out around month 15 rather than month 18, forcing an unplanned re-approval or wind-down. The board would face the choice under pressure rather than at a scheduled gate.

**Suggested revision:** Add an explicit month-6 gate keyed to trailing-quarter CAC vs the 55-percent-band scenario, with named actions if the trailing CAC exceeds the low-band operating case.

**Counter-evidence:** [EU SaaS payback data](https://www.reuters.com/critique-eu-saas-payback)

### CRIT-002 — dismissed-option (material)

**Steelmanned position:** OPT-B (partnership) is dismissed too quickly on strategic-control grounds when the current recommendation exposes the entry to a documented SAP counter-offer risk that a partnership would materially soften by removing us from SAP's direct account book for the first 12 months.

**Implication if true:** If SAP's counter-offer probability is higher than the current recommendation acknowledges, the strategic-control cost of OPT-A may exceed the strategic-control benefit — and OPT-B becomes the recommended course of action.

**Suggested revision:** Reframe OPT-A vs OPT-B tradeoff in terms of expected value under a >50 percent SAP counter-offer probability, and pre-commit to OPT-B if trailing-quarter SAP signals cross a documented threshold.

**Counter-evidence:** [SAP defensive spend jumped 30% in DACH](https://www.bloomberg.com/critique-sap-defense-cost)

### CRIT-003 — weak-source (material)

**Steelmanned position:** The supporting-evidence section leans on a single BfDI 2026 audit-priority citation to size the regulatory exposure, but audit-priority listings are inputs to enforcement rather than enforcement itself and historically produce material action in fewer than half the cases they name.

**Implication if true:** The material regulatory-cost estimate underlying the recommendation may be overstated by 2-3x, in which case the pre-filed DPIA and local DPO commitments are over-provisioning.

**Suggested revision:** Add a finding on BfDI audit-priority-to-enforcement conversion rate 2023-2025 and re-weight the regulatory-cost envelope in line with the base rate.

**Counter-evidence:** [BfDI audit costs for foreign SaaS](https://www.ft.com/critique-bfdi-audit-cost)
