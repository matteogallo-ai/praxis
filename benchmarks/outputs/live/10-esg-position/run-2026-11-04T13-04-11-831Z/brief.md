## Issue Framing

The organisation should file technical comments now in CARB's SB 253 Scope 3 rulemaking and the GHG Protocol consultation, and publish its full position paper only after the Ninth Circuit rules and its inventory review is complete. Advantage: influence over category scope, assurance and methodology versioning is kept, and the greenhushing charge is avoided. Risk: earlier publication would commit the organisation to unverified claims and to rules that may change. Recommendation: conditional support for mandatory Scope 3 disclosure, anchored on IFRS S2 interoperability.

CARB is the deciding body for the immediate step. Its SB 253 Scope 3 design, including a proposed phase-in limited to five of the fifteen GHG Protocol Scope 3 categories, is not final, and CARB was due to decide on Scope 3 assurance by 1 January 2027. The Ninth Circuit's merits ruling on SB 253 is still pending, so the US case should rest on IFRS S2 and investor information needs, not on California law alone. The GHG Protocol's consolidated consultation draft is estimated for Q2 2027, and EU Member States must transpose Omnibus I by 19 March 2027. The organisation's standing rests on its assumed position within at least one mandatory regime.

## Stakeholder Landscape

As of November 2026, regulators are narrowing or proposing to withdraw mandatory Scope 3 requirements, while investors, advocacy groups and the ISSB continue to support them, so rule-making momentum favours scale-back and disclosure demand favours retention.

**Decision-makers.** The Council of the EU signed off an Omnibus that limits CSRD to [companies with more than 1,000 employees and above €450 million net annual turnover](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/24/council-signs-off-simplification-of-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements-to-boost-eu-competitiveness/). The SEC is [proposing to rescind the climate disclosure rules in their entirety](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-49-sec-proposes-rescission-climate-related-disclosure-rules). The SEC's 2024 rule never required Scope 3. CARB staff have proposed that initial SB 253 Scope 3 reporting be [limited to five of the fifteen GHG Protocol Scope 3 categories](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/california-climate-disclosure-laws-carb-finalizes-its-initial-rulemaking-resets-the-2026-deadline-and-previews-the-2027-framework). That design is not final.

**Aligned.** The ISSB amended IFRS S2 in December 2025 so that entities are [permitted to limit measurement and disclosure of Scope 3 Category 15 GHG emissions to financed emissions](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2025/12/issb-issues-targeted-amendments-ifrs-s2/). The amendment adds relief and keeps the requirement. Investor groups have [called on regulators to mandate corporate disclosures of so-called Scope 3 emissions](https://www.allsides.com/news/2024-12-04-0200/environment-investors-95-trillion-seek-mandatory-scope-3-disclosures), though that source is undated. Public Citizen stated that [by cutting Scope 3 disclosures from the rule, the SEC has fallen far short on a core mission](https://www.citizen.org/news/sec-fails-investors-by-declining-to-require-disclosure-of-greenhouse-gas-emissions/).

**Opposed.** [A coalition led by the U.S. Chamber of Commerce challenged the constitutionality of SB 253](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/carb-issues-proposed-regulations-implementing-sb-253-and-sb-261/). Other business associations' 2026 positions were not found.

**Swing voices.** The Ninth Circuit [allowed SB 253 to go into effect](https://clearinghouse.net/case/47320/), but its merits ruling was not confirmed, so California's Scope 3 mandate is not settled. CARB's final rules, the GHG Protocol's [consolidated draft standard for public consultation (estimated Q2 2027)](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/Consolidated-StandardDevelopmentPlan(SDP)-2026.07.29.pdf), and SME suppliers, whose public positions were not found, could each shift the outcome.

## Our Position

The organization holds that, as of this 2026 review, Scope 3 greenhouse gas disclosure belongs in mandatory regimes, and it supports convergence of those regimes on IFRS S2 as their common baseline. The organization holds that the open questions that matter are category scope, the scope and timing of assurance, and a mechanism for updating the GHG Protocol methodology version rather than freezing it, and it acknowledges the protections California's SB 253 already contains. The organization files technical comments on these questions in CARB's 2027 Scope 3 rulemaking and in the GHG Protocol consultation. The organization limits its supplier data requests to the legal scope of the EU value-chain cap and pairs that limit with a funded, voluntary primary-data programme for its largest suppliers by emissions. The organization publishes its full position paper after the Ninth Circuit rules on SB 253's Scope 3 provisions and its own inventory review is complete.

## Rationale

The evidence supports sequenced conditional support for mandatory Scope 3 disclosure: the organisation files technical comments now and publishes the full paper once the Ninth Circuit rules on SB 253 and the internal inventory review concludes.

Regulatory divergence during 2025 and 2026 makes any single-regime stance fragile, so the position anchors on IFRS S2 as the common reference point. The Council of the EU signed off CSRD thresholds of more than 1,000 employees and €450 million turnover, which narrows the EU reporting population ([Council](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/24/council-signs-off-simplification-of-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements-to-boost-eu-competitiveness/)). The SEC proposed on 29 May 2026 to rescind its climate rules in their entirety ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-49-sec-proposes-rescission-climate-related-disclosure-rules)). The ISSB kept the Scope 3 requirement in December 2025 and added targeted relief for Category 15 ([IFRS](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2025/12/issb-issues-targeted-amendments-ifrs-s2/)).

California's Scope 3 mandate is unsettled as of this briefing, so the paper rests on principles rather than SB 253 category counts or a claim that California is the settled US regime. CARB staff proposals from the 21 July 2026 workshop have not been formally adopted ([Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/carbs-july-21-workshop-what-the-proposed-scope-3-and-assurance-rules-mean-for-your-2027-sb-253-report)). The Ninth Circuit allowed SB 253 to take effect, but its merits ruling is pending and could narrow the law ([Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/47320/)).

The GHG Protocol revision, with a consolidated consultation draft estimated for Q2 2027, supports conditions that let the methodology version be updated rather than frozen ([GHG Protocol](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/Consolidated-StandardDevelopmentPlan(SDP)-2026.07.29.pdf)).

Precedent for phase-in and safe-harbour conditions exists, but only as one commissioner's view. Commissioner Crenshaw urged that Scope 3 requirements be phased in for all registrants, with as much safe harbour as possible ([SEC statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/cresnshaw-statement-mandatory-climate-risk-disclosures-030624)). The paper should present this as a single regulator's statement, not as an investor consensus, and should add investor comment letters if they can be sourced.

Supplier burden limits the commitments the organisation can credibly make. The EU value-chain cap confines information requests to partners of up to 1,000 employees to the voluntary standard's content ([Accountancy Europe](https://accountancyeurope.eu/publications/omnibus-explained-key-changes-to-the-csrd-and-csddd/)). The organisation should honour the cap within its legal scope and pair it with a separate, funded, voluntary primary-data programme for its largest suppliers by emissions, so that data quality can improve.

Coherence with the organisation's prior public position is to be confirmed against its published text, which this evidence base does not contain.

## Counter-Arguments Addressed

**Counter-argument 1: mandatory Scope 3 is unworkable, so the organisation should oppose it.** The strongest version notes that Scope 3 figures rest on estimates and that the SEC proposes to rescind its climate rules "in their entirety" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-49-sec-proposes-rescission-climate-related-disclosure-rules)). It concludes that voluntary, materiality-based disclosure is enough. This answer is strong: opposition removes no existing duty, because SB 253, IFRS S2-based regimes and the CSRD still apply where the organisation is in scope. It would also draw investor and NGO criticism. The paper instead seeks phase-in, safe harbour and an updatable methodology version.

**Counter-argument 2: the organisation should publish a full position now, because waiting looks like greenhushing.** Silence is a real risk, and the concern is fair. The answer is moderate-to-strong: the organisation files technical comments now in CARB's rulemaking and the GHG Protocol consultation, whose consolidated draft is estimated for Q2 2027 ([GHG Protocol](https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/Consolidated-StandardDevelopmentPlan(SDP)-2026.07.29.pdf)). It publishes the full paper after the Ninth Circuit rules on SB 253's Scope 3 provisions and the inventory review is complete. Publishing earlier would commit the organisation to claims it has not verified and to rules that may change.

**Counter-argument 3: limiting supplier requests protects small firms but undermines data quality.** Improving purchased-goods data needs supplier-specific primary data, and a light template cannot supply it. The answer is strong, and the paper concedes the point. It confines the commitment to the legal scope of the EU cap, which limits requests to partners with up to 1,000 employees ([Accountancy Europe](https://accountancyeurope.eu/publications/omnibus-explained-key-changes-to-the-csrd-and-csddd/)). It pairs that commitment with a funded, voluntary primary-data programme for the largest suppliers by emissions.

## Adversarial Critique

**Robustness:** low · **Critiques:** 8 (critical=0, material=5, minor=3) · **Revision needed:** yes

**Steelmanned alternative to the current recommendation:** Take a sequenced version of OPT-A. File specific technical comments now in CARB's 2027 Scope 3 rulemaking and the GHG Protocol consultation, covering category scope, the scope of assurance and updating the methodology version. Hold the full public position paper until the Ninth Circuit rules on SB 253's Scope 3 provisions and the inventory review and in-scope test are complete. When the paper is published, it should acknowledge the safe harbour and phased assurance already in SB 253, anchor its case on IFRS S2 interoperability and the organisation's own data roadmap, and use only correctly attributed, current evidence. That keeps the influence and the guard against greenhushing that OPT-A offers, without committing the organisation in public to claims it has not verified or to rules that may change.

### CRIT-001 — hidden-assumption (material)

**Steelmanned position:** OPT-A's main conditions are phase-in, a safe harbour for estimated value-chain data, and limited assurance before reasonable assurance. In California these are already in law. SB 253 already shields good-faith Scope 3 estimates made on a reasonable basis, limits Scope 3 penalties to non-filing until 2030, and starts Scope 3 assurance at the limited level in 2030. If the paper asks CARB for protections the statute already gives, the organisation looks badly informed about the regime it calls its main US reference. The 'conditions' also become empty: they cost the organisation nothing, so a hostile reader can call them window-dressing. The real open questions are category scope, the assurance decision and fixing the methodology version, and the brief treats these only in general terms.

**Implication if true:** The California part of the position has to be rewritten so it acknowledges the protections already in the statute. It should then focus on what is still open: categories, assurance timing and updating the methodology version. Otherwise the stance looks either uninformed or empty.

**Suggested revision:** In 'Our Position' and 'Rationale', state that SB 253 already includes a Scope 3 safe harbour and a phased assurance schedule. Replace the generic safe-harbour ask with specific asks on category phase-in, the scope of assurance and a mechanism for updating the GHG Protocol version.

**Counter-evidence:** [California Climate Disclosure Rules: Your 2027 Guide](https://watershed.com/blog/california-disclosures-a-guide-for-companies)

### CRIT-002 — weak-source (material)

**Steelmanned position:** The brief says investor bodies endorsed phase-in and safe harbour in the SEC rulemaking record. The only source it gives is a statement by one SEC Commissioner, Caroline Crenshaw, made when the 2024 rule was adopted. In that statement she regretted that Scope 3 had been removed entirely. She was not reporting a negotiated investor position. Presenting one commissioner's regret as investor endorsement misattributes the evidence. A hostile reviewer or NGO could use that to discredit the paper's main claim that its conditions improve the rules rather than retreat from them. The same misattribution is OPT-A's headline supporting evidence.

**Implication if true:** The claim that the conditions have precedent becomes much weaker. The paper would need real investor-side evidence, such as investor comment letters or Investor Advisory Committee statements, or it would have to drop the investor-endorsement framing.

**Suggested revision:** Name Commissioner Crenshaw as the source of the SEC statement, remove the wording 'investor bodies themselves endorsed' from the OPT-A rationale, and replace it with cited investor comment letters or drop the claim.

**Counter-evidence:** [Statement by Commissioner Crenshaw on Final Rules Regarding Mandatory Climate Risk Disclosures](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2024/03/07/statement-by-commissioner-crenshaw-on-final-rules-regarding-mandatory-climate-risk-disclosures)

### CRIT-003 — risk-underestimated (material)

**Steelmanned position:** RISK-002 rates the chance that the Ninth Circuit narrows SB 253 as low. At oral argument in January 2026, however, a judge specifically raised concerns about SB 253's Scope 3 requirement and asked about sending it back for a severability analysis. CARB's counsel agreed that would be appropriate. As of November 2026 the ruling is overdue against the window commentators expected. A result that keeps Scopes 1 and 2 but sends back or strikes down Scope 3 is a realistic central scenario, not an outlier. The brief calls California the main mandatory US Scope 3 regime and builds OPT-A's docket comment around CARB's 2027 framework. Both depend on exactly the part of the law the court questioned.

**Implication if true:** RISK-002 should be rated at least medium likelihood. The US part of the position should rest on IFRS S2 and investor information needs, not on SB 253's Scope 3 mandate, and it may be worth timing publication around the ruling.

**Suggested revision:** Raise RISK-002 to medium or high likelihood. Add a fourth pre-approved variant for a ruling that severs or sends back only Scope 3. Remove the claim that California is 'the main mandatory US Scope 3 regime' unless it is qualified by the litigation status.
