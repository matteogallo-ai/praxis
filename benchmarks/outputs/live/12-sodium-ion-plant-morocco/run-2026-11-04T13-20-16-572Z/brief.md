## Complication

Gotion began commissioning an LFP cell gigafactory in Kenitra in [October 2026](https://www.winssolutions.org/gotion-kenitra-battery-gigafactory-morocco/). A Chinese competitor now occupies the Moroccan site a sodium-ion entrant would target. The Industrial Accelerator Act proposal [requires battery cells to be produced in the EU](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/04/european-commission-proposes-industrial-accelerator-act). The act is [not law yet](https://getmilk.tech/blog/how-much-of-your-product-is-made-in-europe), and the Council remains split. Battery Booster loans are [open only to projects located in the EEA](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-seeks-projects-fund-under-battery-booster-facility-2026-07-28_en). A Moroccan plant is therefore outside the €1.5bn interest-free loan facility. No European OEM offtake agreement or sodium-ion model announcement has been found. Sodium-ion still carries a cost premium to LFP in one 2026 analysis. That analysis [expects the cost crossover in 2028–2029](https://earthenergylog.com/articles/lfp-vs-sodium-ion-2026), not earlier. Sources conflict, and none is primary. CATL already has a [sodium-ion car in mass production](https://www.catl.com/en/news/6720.html) with Changan. Committing factory capital before these facts settle risks a stranded asset. The tension is sharp: waiting cedes ground, and building early risks stranded capital.

## Answer

The requester should hold Moroccan factory capital until EU origin rules are final. Within 12 months, it should license a cell design, obtain toll-made samples and sign named buyers. That avoids Gotion-scale exposure, reported at $1.3bn for phase one.

No European OEM sodium-ion demand is documented. EEA-only Battery Booster loans and split Council positions leave Moroccan origin status open. Choose Morocco or Iberia only after a sourced landed-cost benchmark exists.

## Adversarial Critique

**Robustness:** low · **Critiques:** 8 (critical=1, material=6, minor=1) · **Revision needed:** yes

**Steelmanned alternative to the current recommendation:** Adopt a capital-light version of OPT-D. Over the next 12 months, sign a sodium-ion licence or JV, have B-samples made on the licensor's line or by a toll manufacturer so OEM qualification starts now, and lock in named storage and OEM buyers. Commit no factory capital until the Industrial Accelerator Act's origin treatment of free-trade-partner cells is fixed in the final text. Then choose between Morocco and Iberia on a sourced landed-cost and cost-of-capital benchmark. This keeps nearly all of OPT-A's qualification benefit without sinking a pilot of likely several hundred million euros outside the EEA, at a time when the leading Western licensor has just collapsed and CATL is already taking European sodium-ion storage demand.

### CRIT-001 — temporal-blind-spot (critical)

**Steelmanned position:** The brief treats Tiamat as a company building Europe's first sodium-ion gigafactory in Amiens and names it as the first possible licensor for OPT-A. OPT-A requires a licensor that already makes the design at volume and can give ramp-support and yield guarantees. But Tiamat shelved the Amiens project in September 2026 after failing to raise the money. It still has its cells made by subcontractors in China, so it has no volume line of its own that could back yield guarantees. That leaves CATL and HiNa as the only plausible volume licensors. The brief itself classifies them as resistant competitors and says no source shows CATL open to licensing. OPT-A's first dependency may therefore be impossible to meet, and the RISK-010 three-way structure (Tiamat licence, Stellantis offtake, requester builds) is based on an outdated picture.

**Implication if true:** If no non-competing licensor with volume production can be signed, OPT-A turns into an unlicensed new-entrant build, which is the Northvolt pattern the brief warns against, or into dependence on a Chinese rival's IP. In either case the recommended option loses its main way of reducing risk.

**Suggested revision:** Update the Tiamat stakeholder entry and RISK-010, and make a signed licence term sheet with yield guarantees a precondition for any pilot spend, not something negotiated alongside it.

**Counter-evidence:** [L'usine devait créer jusqu'à 2000 emplois : incapable de réunir les fonds nécessaires, la start-up Tiamat abandonne son projet de gigafactory de batteries sodium-ion dans la Somme](https://www.usinenouvelle.com/energie/stockage/batteries/lusine-devait-creer-jusqua-2000-emplois-incapable-de-reunir-les-fonds-necessaires-la-start-up-tiamat-abandonne-son-projet-de-gigafactory-de-batteries-sodium-ion-dans-la-somme.5JECNCELKRGRJNAJDEY2C5LX7M.html)

### CRIT-002 — risk-underestimated (material)

**Steelmanned position:** OPT-A and the RISK-002 mitigations rely on stationary-storage buyers for early pilot revenue and on at least 25% non-automotive offtake as a demand hedge. CATL is already locking up European sodium-ion storage demand: a 3 GWh agreement with Intilion in the DACH region, a 5 GWh plan with Alfen, and international deliveries planned from June 2027. A 1-2 GWh new entrant would compete for the same integrators against a scale incumbent. The Industrial Accelerator Act's storage-origin rules would also apply to publicly supported projects. The storage hedge is therefore weaker than the brief assumes, and the brief rates the residual risk only medium.

**Implication if true:** Pilot revenue and the 25% secondary-offtake hedge could fall short. That makes the pilot a pure cost centre and raises the chance that the gates fail with the capital already spent.

**Suggested revision:** Add CATL's European sodium-ion storage deals to RISK-004 and RISK-002, and require named storage buyers with pricing before the pilot is approved, not after.

**Counter-evidence:** [CATL, Intilion target up to 3 GWh of sodium-ion storage in Europe - pv magazine Global](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/13/catl-intilion-target-up-to-3-gwh-of-sodium-ion-storage-in-europe/)

### CRIT-003 — temporal-blind-spot (material)

**Steelmanned position:** The lithium gate in OPT-A, RISK-003 and So What (a positive IRR at CNY 100,000/t or lower) is presented as a stress test, but it is roughly today's spot price, not a downside case. Chinese carbonate traded around CNY 72,000/t at the end of 2025, rose above CNY 150,000 in January 2026 and is now around CNY 104,000. A real downside test should use late-2025 levels. The 2026 swings also point to something the brief misses entirely: OEMs may value sodium-ion as a hedge against lithium volatility even without an average cost advantage. The brief does not model that rationale either for or against the plant.

**Implication if true:** The cost gate could be passed on a near-spot assumption and still leave the plant uneconomic if lithium goes back to 2025 lows. Separately, the volatility-hedge value could be a stronger selling point to OEMs than cost.

**Suggested revision:** Reset the IRR gate to CNY 70,000-75,000/t, and add a scenario that values sodium-ion as a hedge against lithium volatility in OEM interviews.

**Counter-evidence:** [Lithium Prices Analysis Retreat as Chinese Carbonate Falls From Its 2026 Peak](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/lithium-prices-analysis-retreat-as-chinese-carbonate-falls-from-its-2026-peak)

### CRIT-004 — overconfidence (material)

**Steelmanned position:** The Answer says OPT-A caps exposure at pilot scale and compares it with Gotion's $1.3bn first phase. But the brief itself notes that no capex figure exists for the pilot, and 1-2 GWh is more than many sodium-ion players' first commercial phase. Tiamat's Amiens project was estimated at about €500m for 5 GWh, and it collapsed because it could not close a first phase of nearly €200m, with half the money coming from public grants. A 1-2 GWh line built by a newcomer outside the EEA could plausibly need several hundred million euros. Calling that exposure 'capped' or a 'small share' is not supported.

**Implication if true:** The board would be approving a nine-figure commitment that it believes is small. A true pilot below 0.5 GWh, or capacity bought from a toll manufacturer, could provide samples at a fraction of the cost.

**Suggested revision:** Give a sourced capex range for 0.3, 1 and 2 GWh pilot options in the Answer and So What, and size the pilot to sample needs rather than to a fixed 1-2 GWh.

**Counter-evidence:** [Batterie Sodium-Ion à la française : pourquoi Tiamat a renoncé à son usine de batteries près d'Amiens](https://www.generation-nt.com/actualites/tiamat-batterie-sodium-ion-gigafactory-report-2081353)

### CRIT-005 — risk-underestimated (material)

**Steelmanned position:** The RISK-001 mitigation and the OPT-A gate ask for a binding customs-origin opinion under the EU-Morocco Association Agreement. That opinion covers preferential origin, meaning tariff treatment. The Industrial Accelerator Act's Union-origin test, by contrast, is set under the Union Customs Code's non-preferential rules, under which cells made in Morocco are Moroccan, not Union, origin. The gate could therefore be passed while the cells still fail the vehicle-level rule that cells be of Union origin. Inclusion would need a change to the law, which France and Germany oppose. On the current text, exclusion is the base case, not a medium-likelihood risk.

**Implication if true:** The origin gate as written gives false comfort. EV channels with public support could be closed to Moroccan cells just as the plant ramps up, because the cell-origin phase begins about six months after the Act enters into force.

**Suggested revision:** Change the gate to require a final Industrial Accelerator Act text, or a Council general approach, that explicitly treats free-trade partners' cells as Union origin, and raise RISK-001 likelihood to high.

**Counter-evidence:** [EN EN EUROPEAN COMMISSION Brussels, XXX \[…\](2026) XXX draft Proposal for a](https://table.media/assets/entwurf-iaa-proposal.pdf)

### CRIT-006 — dismissed-option (material)

**Steelmanned position:** OPT-D was set aside mainly because OEMs will not qualify cells from a supplier without a line. But OPT-A itself depends on a licensed, already-industrialised design. B-samples can come from the licensor's existing line or a toll manufacturer, which is exactly how Tiamat has supplied cells, through Zenergy in China, without any plant of its own. That would start OEM qualification without committing capital to Morocco before the origin rules and cost crossover are settled. The brief's argument that deferral 'cedes 12-18 months' also applies to OPT-A, because its own gates push volume supply to 2029-2030 anyway.

**Implication if true:** OPT-D, or a capital-light hybrid with toll-made samples, would capture most of OPT-A's qualification benefit while avoiding the sunk pilot capex and the origin exposure.

**Suggested revision:** Add a hybrid OPT-D option (licence plus toll-manufactured B-samples plus origin tracking) and compare it directly with OPT-A on cost and qualification timing.

**Counter-evidence:** [Tiamat Energy : batteries sodium-ion et gigafactory d'Amiens](https://www.mobilityinthecity.com/f/tiamat-energy/)

### CRIT-007 — internal-contradiction (material)

**Steelmanned position:** Part of the case against OPT-C is that it 'gives up Moroccan cost and incentive advantages'. Yet the same options artefact and its unresolved uncertainties say no capex, unit-cost or payback benchmark between Morocco and Iberia was found. The brief is ranking options on an advantage it says it cannot quantify. Meanwhile, OPT-C is the only option that removes RISK-001, RISK-006 and RISK-008 at once. And ACEA's own study says EU rules of origin remain ambiguous about whether near-shoring partners like Morocco count as equivalent.

**Implication if true:** If the Moroccan cost advantage is small or is outweighed by origin and financing penalties, OPT-C, or an Iberian site for the pilot itself, should rank at least equal to OPT-A.

**Suggested revision:** Commission a Morocco-versus-Iberia landed-cost and cost-of-capital comparison before choosing the pilot site, and remove the unquantified 'cost advantage' wording from the rationale until it is done.

**Counter-evidence:** [Industrial Accelerator Act: industry may fail to meet battery demand in EU - electrive.com](https://www.electrive.com/2026/09/29/industrial-accelerator-act-industry-may-fail-to-meet-battery-demand-in-eu/)

### CRIT-008 — temporal-blind-spot (minor)

**Steelmanned position:** The Stellantis entry rests on an undated Reuters item about its Tiamat stake. More recent evidence shows Stellantis anchoring its European cell supply with CATL through a 50 GWh LFP plant in Spain, and Tiamat's own French plant is now shelved. CATL, which already mass-produces sodium-ion Naxtra cells, is therefore the more likely route for any Stellantis sodium-ion volumes. That further weakens the idea that a Moroccan newcomer could become Stellantis's anchor sodium-ion supplier.

**Implication if true:** This confirms that RISK-010 is already high likelihood and calls for a non-Stellantis anchor strategy, but it does not change the ranking on its own.

**Suggested revision:** Update the Stellantis stakeholder entry with the CATL Spain partnership and the Tiamat suspension, and widen RISK-010's mitigation to give equal priority to non-Stellantis anchors.
