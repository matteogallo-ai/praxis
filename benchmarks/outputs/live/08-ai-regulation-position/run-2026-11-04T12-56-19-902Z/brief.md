## Issue Framing

The company should adopt a compliance-first public position, published only after Article 50 readiness is confirmed ahead of the 2 December 2026 watermarking date. The advantage is the lowest reputational exposure and the strongest trust signal. The risk is that public commitments outrun delivery. The recommendation is to hold publication until the Article 50 and Code of Practice decisions are settled.

The legislative file is closed. The Council gave final approval to the AI simplification regulation on 29 June 2026 ([Council press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/)). The Commission reports that the Omnibus entered into force on 27 July 2026 and set high-risk dates of 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028 ([Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act)). Open decisions now sit with the European Commission, including the AI Office, which issues remaining guidance and enforces general-purpose AI obligations, and with Member State authorities responsible for national enforcement. Article 50 duties apply from 2 August 2026, and law-firm reporting places the watermarking obligation at 2 December 2026 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-agrees-digital-omnibus-deal-simplify-ai-rules)).

The company's standing rests on its role as developer, provider or deployer within the Act's scope, which remains to be confirmed. Because the dates are fixed, implementation requests belong in technical consultation responses rather than the headline text.

## Stakeholder Landscape

The EU institutions that decide the file, the Commission, the Council and Parliament, are aligned behind the Digital Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026, so momentum favours the amended timetable while industry associations ask for more and civil-society groups object.

**Decision-makers.** The Commission set the high-risk dates at [2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act). The Council gave [final approval on 29 June 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/). Parliament adopted the package [with 423 votes in favour, 57 against and 174 abstentions](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/06/european-parliament-pauses-ai-act-and-delays-high-risk-systems/). The legislative text is closed, so remaining decisions sit in guidance, standards and national enforcement.

**Aligned, with conditions.** Bitkom welcomed the deal and states that ["Auch beim AI Act bleibt umfangreicher Verbesserungsbedarf"](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Bitkom-Digitaler-Omnibus-EU), meaning it wants further improvement. eco asks that the Omnibus [deliver less bureaucracy and more legal certainty](https://www.eco.de/presse/eco-zum-digital-omnibus-europa-laesst-zentrale-ki-huerden-bestehen/). Its criticism targets remaining burdens, not the Act as a whole.

**Opposed.** Liberties states that the adopted text ["will weaken AI Act protections, delay the enforcement of key provisions, and empower industry actors"](https://www.liberties.eu/en/stories/ai-omnibus-ep-vote/45715). The EDPB and EDPS [raised concern that the different applicable legal instruments could lead to legal uncertainty](https://www.delorscentre.eu/en/publications/detail/publication/the-eus-digital-and-ai-omnibus).

**Swing voices.** The TÜV-Verband [rated the agreement ambivalently](https://www.datensicherheit.de/ki-omnibus-bitkom-kommentar-kompromiss-eu-trilog-verhandlung-ai-act). It welcomed the longer deadlines and the deepfake ban but criticised the carve-out of high-risk applications. CEN-CENELEC's public position is not documented, though its standards timetable was the stated reason for the postponement. Employee and union positions are also undocumented. In the options analysis, a compliance-first posture drew supportive reactions from digital-rights groups and the TÜV-Verband and a resistant reaction from Bitkom.

## Our Position

The organization holds that the EU AI Act, as amended by the Digital Omnibus, is the binding standard for its AI systems from today through the final application date of 2 August 2028, and it commits to meet each obligation as it falls due without asking for further delay or dilution. The organization complies with the Article 50 transparency obligations that apply from 2 August 2026, completes the marking and labelling of AI-generated content by 2 December 2026 under the ownership of its Chief Technology Officer, and signs the [Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content). The organization dates its high-risk commitments to the deadlines now in force, [2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for AI embedded in regulated products](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act), and ties them to the availability of harmonised standards, building its internal controls on the stable requirements of Articles 9 to 15 in the meantime. The organization classifies its systems under the Commission's final Article 6 guidelines and reviews that classification within 90 days of their publication. The organization directs its input on implementation to technical channels, namely consultation responses, standards committee work and consistent national enforcement, and makes no public request to reopen the Act.

## Rationale

The legal position rests on dates that are now fixed, so the company's case lies in compliance rather than in further delay. The Digital Omnibus moved the high-risk deadlines to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for AI embedded in products ([Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act)). Commentary calls the change "a deferral rather than a dismantling" of the Act's architecture ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

Obligations already in force support a compliance-first stance today. Article 50 duties apply from 2 August 2026, with the watermarking duty postponed to 2 December 2026 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-agrees-digital-omnibus-deal-simplify-ai-rules)). The AI Office holds enforcement powers over GPAI models ([Commission AI Act page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)). The Commission treats the Article 50 requirements as legal obligations ([Transparency Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)). Signing a Code of Practice gives providers more legal certainty than other routes to proof ([GPAI Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai)). The company therefore confirms Article 50 readiness and decides on the Transparency Code before it publishes.

Pending standards and guidelines justify dated, conditional commitments through 2027. The postponement came from the unavailability of harmonised standards ([CEN-CENELEC](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2026/newsletter/ots-77-etuc/)), and the Article 6 classification guidelines are due by 1 August 2027 ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/)). The company anchors its controls to Articles 9-15, ties standards-based commitments to Official Journal citation, and commits to classify its systems within 90 days of the final guidelines.

Reputational coherence points the same way over the next two to three years. Digital-rights groups argue the Omnibus "weakens fundamental rights protections" ([Liberties](https://www.liberties.eu/en/stories/ai-omnibus-ep-vote/45715)), so public asks for dilution would carry high cost and little leverage on a closed legislative file. The company therefore leads with commitments and sends its implementation asks on Article 6, standards and enforcement consistency through technical consultation responses and JTC 21 work, not through the headline.

Consistency with the company's prior public position is a condition of release. Its earlier AI statements and its Bitkom and eco commitments must be confirmed by Legal as uncontradicted by this paper before publication.

## Counter-Arguments Addressed

**Compliance-first, no public asks.** Critics argue that the company earns the most trust and carries the least reputational exposure by committing to compliance now and voicing implementation concerns only through technical channels. This paper adopts most of that view. It leads with dated compliance commitments, makes Article 50 readiness and a named-owner decision on the Transparency Code of Practice conditions for publication, and keeps asks out of the headline. It sends them through consultation responses and standards committees instead. The company still keeps those asks, because the Article 6 classification guidelines and national enforcement consistency remain unsettled, and a company that stays silent cannot shape them.

**Endorsement legitimises a rollback.** Digital-rights groups hold that the amended Act will "weaken AI Act protections, delay the enforcement of key provisions, and empower industry actors" ([Liberties](https://www.liberties.eu/en/stories/ai-omnibus-ep-vote/45715)). That concern is fair. The company asks for no further delay and no reopening of the text, and it pairs each ask with a public commitment. Law-firm commentary also describes the Omnibus as "a deferral rather than a dismantling" ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)), so the core obligations stand.

**More delay is still needed.** Industry coalitions argue that incomplete standards justify a further pause. The Commission has already held to the legal timeline, "dismissing calls from some companies and countries for a pause" ([SRN News](https://srnnews.com/artificial-intelligence-rules-to-go-ahead-no-pause-eu-commission-says/)). The Omnibus has since moved the high-risk dates. A new request would carry little weight and would invite the reputational exposure described above. The company therefore ties its commitments to standards availability instead of seeking more time.

## Adversarial Critique

**Steelmanned alternative to the current recommendation:** Adopt an OPT-C-led hybrid. Publish a compliance-first position. It would confirm Article 50 compliance and sign the Transparency Code (joining about 190 peers), commit to an EN 18286-based quality management system now that it reportedly has presumption of conformity, and commit to classification under the final Article 6 guidelines within 90 days of their adoption. Besides avoiding public asks aimed at a consultation that has already closed, this gives the strongest trust signal at the lowest reputational exposure while keeping the influence that still exists.

### CRIT-001 — temporal-blind-spot (critical)

**Steelmanned position:** The brief aims its main ask at Article 6 high-risk classification guidelines that it says are due by 1 August 2027. That window has very likely already passed. The Commission published draft Article 6 guidelines on 19 May 2026 and ran a targeted consultation that was extended to 23 July 2026. It says the final guidelines will be adopted by the end of 2026. The 1 August 2027 date appears to mix up the Article 6 guidelines with separate guidance on Articles 8(2), 9(10) and 17(3) for Annex I products. If so, OPT-A's main channel of influence is a consultation that closed more than three months ago. The paper would be published around the time the guidelines are finalised, so it would reach the Commission too late to matter and would make the company look poorly informed about the file it claims to be engaging on.

**Implication if true:** OPT-A's main reason for keeping public asks, and its main advantage over OPT-C, mostly falls away. The paper would need new targets for its asks: how the final guidelines are applied, delegated acts that amend the Article 6(3) conditions, the standards still pending, and national enforcement.

**Suggested revision:** Replace the 1 August 2027 Article 6 framing in issue-framing, rationale and counter-arguments-addressed with the Commission's actual timeline (draft 19 May 2026, consultation closed 23 July 2026, final guidelines by end of 2026), and confirm whether the company filed a response.
